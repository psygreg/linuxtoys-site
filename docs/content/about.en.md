# About this project
### A letter to you

When I started my Linux journey, contrary to many believe, it was **far** from easy or straightforward. For a start, I had a *Nvidia* graphics card back in the day, which already made for some very tricky situations. It was early 2024, and while the experience had already improved significantly from what it was before, it has built up and is still gaining significant momentum to this day - *LinuxToys* most definitely being a part of that momentum to its users. Still, I didn't give up despite all hardships. The reason I didn't was that ultimately, for the first time, I felt like my computer was *actually* mine - and there's nothing quite like that feeling.

In a world increasingly dominated by *"enshittification"*, that deeply resonated with me and made me want to give something in return to this community.  

Fast-forward to the late 2024, and I started making my own *bash* scripts. A lot of mistakes, trial and error and looking at code snippets later, I found out I actually enjoyed it. Several scripts later, I started trying to figure out other people's problems as a way to challenge myself. Slowly but surely, a collection of scripts piled up, and that's how *LinuxToys* was born - or at least, what it used to be.

Its core principle is simple in theory: instead of making yet another distro to solve a perceived problem, I made a standard solution that can follow you anywhere you go. This, however, would prove to be **far** more difficult in practice. From different packaging, to all kinds of quirks, there were many obstacles in the way, but with each one of those, I learned something valuable. The *LinuxToys* of today is an amalgamation of all this experience acquired both with Linux systems and programming itself. 

As this knowledge accumulated, I soon understood that **software distribution may be one of, if not the biggest problem on the Linux ecosystem**. Between licensing and delivery, there was a pattern on the ways users that just wanted to get work done felt let down. Many people go after those users, attempting to invalidate their experience, resulting in the stereotypical view many have about the Linux community. While in the beginning I would be one of those apologists, this changed as soon as the current vision for *LinuxToys* started taking shape. I wanted to stop making excuses, and start bridging that gap myself, as a hands-on approach and admittedly quite impatient kind of guy. 

In the effort of simplifying software delivery, we've built together a platform where developers can ensure their app will work like magic for thousands of users, anywhere they want to deliver it. Developer or user, I hope you find it ever more useful in the years to come.
